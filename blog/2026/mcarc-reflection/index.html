Reflecting on my service to MCARC

Over two years ago I got my amateur radio license. Little did I know right after passing my exam, that this was the beginning of a journey that would lead me down a path I never thought I would go down. After I passed my test I got on a local repeater and started trying to talk to someone. This was quite the challenge for a myriad of reasons. Eventually I was able to make contact with Gary Sherman W9GNR. Gary invited me to the club meeting that night, so I thought "why not" and attended. This is where things start to get interesting.

This meeting was miserable. Vote on this, motion for that. Not really that related to radio. This was not what I expected the club would be. Furthremore, it was not all that welcoming adding to my feeling of being out of place. Afterwards, I went to dinner with my family, and I vividly recall saying that I didn't want to be a part of the club. After a little more consideration, I decided that I would give it another shot. But not the monthy evening meeting; I wanted to try the weekly morning donut meeting.

The morning donut meeting was a completely different experience. It was much more casual, friendly, and focused on radio activities. I felt more comfortable and it became clear to me that this was the kind of environment where I could thrive as a member of the club. From that point on, I started attending the morning meetings regularly. One thing I left out earlier; this was not my first experience with amateur radio. When I was eight, I had a weather balloon project I wanted to do, and amateur radio was a natural part of that. As such, I studied and took the test. While I did not pass and became engaged in other youth pastimes, it never fully left my mind. Before_I lost interest as a child,_I talked to and learned from Jeff Stewart AK9JS as he is a family friend deeply involved in amateur radio. Fast forward back to the donut meetings, and once again there Jeff was, teaching me more things.

I got my general not long after my technician, then a few months later got my extra class license. THrough that time I was learning from Jeff and others about radio. However, through all of this time there was one thought that would not leave my head.
"If I really had to push myself to become involved and not feel like an outsider, how many people are being pushed out of the hobby because they didn't like their first meeting and now made to feel this way."

To challenge myself to do more work in solving this issue and to share my findings with others, I did what any reasonable person would do; make a powerpoint.
